# Cluely Playbook — Voice Template

## TL;DR (5 lines)
1. Open with a personal-stake claim that sounds like trouble (got kicked out, got banned, lost the offer).
2. Tie the trouble to a system you think is broken (Leetcode, gatekeepers, AI gold-rush noise).
3. Reveal the product as your reaction to that system, not as a launch.
4. Hand the audience a side to pick. Polarized > liked.
5. End with one ask, one link. No "please retweet" energy — let the controversy do the asking.

## What Cluely actually did (the moves to copy)

### 1. The viral launch moment
On **2025-03-26**, Roy Lee posted an X thread that opened: *"I just got kicked out of Columbia for taking a stand against Leetcode interviews. Here's the whole story (long thread)"* ([x.com/im_roy_lee/status/1905063484783472859](https://x.com/im_roy_lee/status/1905063484783472859)). He cross-posted the same story to LinkedIn the same day — that LinkedIn post pulled **3,000+ comments**. The Interview Coder tool he was suspended for racked up **~250 million impressions** across the campaign. The pattern: he didn't say "I built a thing." He said *"I got punished for the thing"* — and let the audience ask what the thing was. The hook was conflict, not feature.

### 2. The Columbia controversy lever
The arc was real, not scripted, but every retelling was **shaped**. Sequence:
1. Build tool ("Interview Coder") that helps you pass Leetcode interviews invisibly.
2. Use it on an Amazon interview, get an offer, **post about it on LinkedIn** ([linkedin.com/posts/roy-lee-goat_i-got-kicked-out-of-columbia-for-building...](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/roy-lee-goat_i-got-kicked-out-of-columbia-for-building-activity-7319827343730974720-f4ew)).
3. Amazon rescinds. Columbia opens an academic-integrity case. Suspended for one year.
4. Re-frame: not "I cheated" but "**I took a stand against a broken hiring ritual**" (this single re-frame is the entire trick).
5. Rebrand Interview Coder → Cluely on **2025-04-20** with a launch film of him on a date using AR glasses to fake his age. Caption: "**cheat on everything**." Result: **7.8M+ views, ~20K likes, ~3.4K retweets** on the launch tweet ([cluely.com/blog/virality](https://cluely.com/blog/virality)).
6. 70,000 signups in week 1; $5.3M seed from Abstract + Susa announced 2025-04-21 ([techcrunch.com/2025/04/21/...](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/21/columbia-student-suspended-over-interview-cheating-tool-raises-5-3m-to-cheat-on-everything/)); $15M Series A from a16z on 2025-06-20 ([techcrunch.com/2025/06/20/cluely-a-startup-that-helps-cheat-on-everything-raises-15m-from-a16z](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/20/cluely-a-startup-that-helps-cheat-on-everything-raises-15m-from-a16z/)).

The lever wasn't the suspension. It was that he **published the suspension before anyone else could**. He owned the narrative timing.

### 3. X cadence Apr-Oct 2025
- **Frequency:** multiple posts/day on @im_roy_lee, sometimes hourly during news cycles.
- **Length:** 80% are <100 chars. The viral thread was the exception, not the rule.
- **Format wins:** one-liners that frame an enemy. Examples preserved across coverage: *"today is the start of a world where you never have to think again"* (Cluely launch tweet, June 2025); *"we just killed 9 industries"*; *"how you feel about Cluely is how you feel about yourself"* (deleted by him for being too inflammatory — i.e. it worked); *"Cluely's aura is just too strong!"* (after SFPD shut down a Cluely party, [techcrunch.com/2025/06/17/police-shut-down-cluelys-party](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/17/police-shut-down-cluelys-party-the-cheat-at-everything-startup/)).
- **Replies:** he never punches down; he punches across. He picks fights with category leaders (Leetcode, OpenAI, "the AI noise") not with random replies.
- His own self-description (TechCrunch Disrupt 2025, [techcrunch.com/2025/10/29/cluelys-roy-lee-on-the-ragebait-strategy-for-startup-marketing](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/29/cluelys-roy-lee-on-the-ragebait-strategy-for-startup-marketing/)): *"I don't ragebait, I'm just honest and authentic and people see that as ragebaiting."*

### 4. TikTok / video strategy
- **Format split:** one prestige long-form launch film (~90s, 6-figure production cost), then thousands of **<30s clips** cut from it by a clipper army (Cluely now employs ~700 clippers per Roy Lee's TechCrunch Disrupt talk).
- **Hook structure:** first 1.5 seconds = a frame that looks illegal. Example launch film opens on him at dinner with text overlay "I'm wearing smart glasses on this date" before anything else happens.
- **On-screen text:** big, sans-serif, often white-on-black or burned into the frame, restating the verbal hook so muted-autoplay viewers still get the joke.
- **Captions style:** lowercase, no period at the end, one sentence. Mirrors the X voice exactly.
- **Volume play:** total Cluely social impressions crossed **1 billion** within months (Sourcery, Sept 2025 — [sourcery.vc/p/cluelys-mega-viral-launch](https://www.sourcery.vc/p/cluelys-mega-viral-launch)). They optimize for total surface area, not per-clip polish.

### 5. Attention-to-revenue pipeline
- **Top of funnel:** controversy → free download → desktop overlay you can try in 30 seconds.
- **Pricing page anchor:** Free tier (limited weekly minutes) → **~$20/mo Pro** for unlimited responses → Enterprise custom. The $20 anchor is deliberate — same price as ChatGPT Plus, removing the price-comparison objection.
- **Fomo trigger on landing:** the v1 cluely.com hero was just the words *"cheat on everything"* + a button. No countdown, no waitlist. The fomo wasn't manufactured — it was **the news cycle itself**. Each press hit drove the next sign-up spike.
- **Honest caveat:** Roy Lee later admitted he inflated the public ARR figures ($7M claim, [techcrunch.com/2026/03/05/cluely-ceo-roy-lee-admits-to-publicly-lying-about-revenue-numbers-last-year](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/05/cluely-ceo-roy-lee-admits-to-publicly-lying-about-revenue-numbers-last-year/)). So we don't actually know real conversion. Treat the funnel theory as plausible, not proven.

### 6. Why Cluely beat incumbents
The incumbents (LeetCode prep tools, existing AI interview helpers) had **better products**. Cluely had **a story you could retell at dinner in one sentence**: "the kid Columbia kicked out for cheating Amazon." That sentence is the entire moat for the first 90 days. The non-obvious lever wasn't the product; it was that the founder volunteered to be the antagonist of his own origin story — and the press could not resist. Distribution > product, *for the launch window only*. (By Nov 2025, Roy himself was telling TechCrunch "viral hype is not enough," [techcrunch.com/2025/11/05/cluelys-roy-lee-hints-that-viral-hype-is-not-enough](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/05/cluelys-roy-lee-hints-that-viral-hype-is-not-enough/) — confirming the playbook expires.)

## 8. Anti-patterns — what we do NOT copy from Cluely

1. **Never frame the product around dishonesty.** Roy Lee can survive "cheat on everything" because he's a 21-year-old undergrad with no career to protect. Alex is an ML/SDE founder who VCs and potential enterprise customers will Google. A "cheat" framing on VibeXForge would torch a16z-tier conversations. Our adjacent move is *"underdog vs gatekeeper"* — not *"liar vs honest people."*
2. **Never inflate metrics.** Cluely's ARR scandal (admitted lie about $7M, [techcrunch.com/2026/03/05/...](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/05/cluely-ceo-roy-lee-admits-to-publicly-lying-about-revenue-numbers-last-year/)) is the single thing that nearly killed them post-Series A. Our numbers (build cost, signup counts, project counts) must be **independently verifiable from the live site**. Always.
3. **Never punch down.** Cluely's controversy targets institutions (Columbia, Leetcode, "the AI industry"). Avoid mocking individual creators, competitors by name, or any specific user. Punch up or punch across.
4. **Don't hire 700 clippers.** That's a $15M-Series-A-budget move. Our analog: write 1 launch film, **manually** cut 5–8 clips for X / 小红书 / Threads / TikTok / Reels, ship over 2 weeks, see which arm the bandit favors.
5. **Don't lean on "I got kicked out / fired / banned"** unless it actually happened. Inventing the suspension story would be plagiarism *and* easily falsifiable. Use real frictions Alex actually has: the Vercel bill, the timezone problem, the GitHub stars-without-paying-users gap.
6. **Don't violate platform ToS.** 小红书 deletes posts that name competitors negatively, posts with suspected paid promotion not flagged as 广告, or posts that look "诱导分享." Keep the ask soft. TikTok will throttle anything that explicitly says "cheat" or "hack" — if we use the Cluely arc, the on-screen text should say "the rail they ignored," not "the system you can beat."
7. **Don't reuse this voice on /investors or in a VC deck.** It will read as unserious. Bandit should exclude `cluely-playbook` for `linkedin_vc_facing` and `hacker_news_show_hn` arms — see `applies_to` / `do_not_use_for` in the frontmatter.
8. **Don't expect this to work twice in a row.** Controversy decays. If `cluely-playbook` wins one post via the bandit, the next post should default back to `stat-led` or `build-in-public` to avoid audience fatigue. (Marketing-agent's bandit handles this automatically via Beta updates, but the human reviewer should still eyeball it.)
